## Design Context

### Users
Operations professionals in cannabis cultivation and processing: trim managers, cultivation managers, lab managers (extraction + manufacturing). Owners may access reports or use as owner/operators. These are busy, hands-on people who need to move fast during active sessions — tech comfort is moderate to high. They're managing crews, weights, and timelines in real-time.

### Brand Personality
**Clean. Smart. Intuitive.**

The interface should feel like a premium operations tool — the kind of software that earns trust on first use. Think Apple-level simplicity applied to agricultural operations. Every interaction should feel fast, confident, and obvious.

**Emotional goals:** Confidence, control, calm efficiency, speed.

**Tagline:** "Elevating Harvest Efficiency"

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Light mode only** — clean, bright, professional. No dark app chrome.
- **Primary:** Emerald (#10B981) — represents flower output, primary actions
- **Semantic colors:** Amber (shake), Blue (trim), Red (waste) — these are domain-meaningful and should stay
- **Anti-references:** No stoner/cannabis aesthetic whatsoever. No leaf motifs, no green-everything, no counter-culture vibes. Nothing that reveals this is cannabis at first glance. No enterprise-heavy UI (SAP, Oracle). No cluttered dashboards.
- **Target feel:** Apple product — minimal, purposeful, quietly confident. The kind of tool where you don't notice the design because it just works.

### Design Principles

1. **Invisible by design** — The best interface is one users don't think about. Reduce friction, remove decoration, let the data and actions speak. Every element must earn its place.

2. **Speed is a feature** — These users are managing active operations. Interactions should feel instant. Optimize for the fewest clicks/taps to complete common tasks. Progressive disclosure over overwhelming dashboards.

3. **Quiet confidence** — No flashy gradients, no glowing accents, no visual noise. Use whitespace, typography, and subtle hierarchy to communicate professionalism. The interface should feel like it was made by someone who knows what they're doing.

4. **Domain-honest, brand-neutral** — Use industry terminology (flower, shake, trim, waste) without cannabis branding. The product should look at home in any agricultural or manufacturing operations context.

5. **Data clarity over data density** — Present numbers, weights, and metrics with precision and readability. Tabular nums, clear labels, logical grouping. When in doubt, show less with more clarity rather than more with less.
