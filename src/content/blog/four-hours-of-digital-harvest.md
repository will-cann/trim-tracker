Harvest day is the most chaotic day in the cycle. Everyone is in the room, everything is moving, and the only thing that matters is getting the plants down and hung so the room can turn. The records come second.

Then the second part of harvest starts, and nobody counts it as harvest. Our compliance administrator would sit down with the sheets and do what we called digital harvest: plant tag by plant tag, weight by weight, typed into the system and into METRC. About four hours, every time. By the end his brain was numb and the error rate said so.

We tried the obvious thing first. Before any of this, we had built systems to collect every data point a facility produces — the goal was a supply chain that could optimize itself off its own numbers. What we found was that the data arrived inconsistently filled and barely structured, because collection was a separate step from the work. People do the work. The step after the work is where the record dies.

> If capturing the record is a second task, it does not get done well. It has to happen in the same motion as the thing being recorded.

That reframed the problem. The four hours were not a typing problem; they were a timing problem. The weight existed for one moment — on the scale, with the plant in front of you — and we were trying to reconstruct it later from paper. Everything downstream of that moment is reconciliation.

So the mechanism has to live at the scale. The first version was a scanner: scan the tag, two taps, enter the number, next plant. Even two taps are too many for a wet glove; the fastest input a person has on harvest day is their voice. The number gets said out loud anyway; the system should be the one writing it down.

Two things follow for any facility:

- **Time the second harvest.** Hours spent entering harvest after harvest is a real cost. Write it next to the chop date.
- **Move the record to the moment.** Whatever gets weighed, flagged, or counted should be captured where and when it happens, by the person doing it. Anything else is four hours later.

How we handle it: Harvest Day was the second thing we built, and it exists to take that afternoon to zero.
