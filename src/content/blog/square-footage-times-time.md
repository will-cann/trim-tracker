A vertically integrated operator we worked at brought in a CFO from Wall Street. He built the financial plan the way you would expect: rooms, cycles per year, grams per cycle, price. The plan was fine. The floor was not. Rooms took about a week to turn over after harvest, and at the end of the year nobody could explain why the room count and the cycle count did not multiply out the way the spreadsheet said they would.

The explanation is one line. **Your money is square footage times time.** A flower room earns only while plants are in it. Every day between the last plant out and the first plant in is a day of rent on a room that produces nothing.

## The formula

Cycles per year, per room:

365 ÷ (flower days + turnover days)

Turnover is everything from the chop to the next planting: harvest, teardown, clean, reset. Harvest is the part you control most directly on the day.

## Worked example

A 63-day flower cycle (nine weeks), one room:

| Turnover | Days per cycle | Cycles per year | Lost vs. 1-day turn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 day | 64 | 5.70 | — |
| 2 days | 65 | 5.62 | 1.5% |
| 4 days | 67 | 5.45 | 4.5% |
| 7 days | 70 | 5.21 | 8.6% |

A week-long turn costs about half a cycle per room per year — roughly 8–9% of that room's annual output. Multiply by the number of flower rooms and it is usually the difference between the CFO's number and the actual one.

## The input people get wrong

The plan assumes a turnover of a day or two because that is what everyone says they do. The floor's real number is whatever it took last time, and nobody wrote it down. Measure it: chop date to planting date, per room, every cycle. If you have never measured it, assume it is worse than you think.

Getting harvest done in a day is not a point of pride. It is the line item.
