Ask an extraction lead how big a batch is and the answer is never "whatever came off the wash." It is 500 grams, or 1,000, or 1,500. Round numbers, every time.

The reason is the lab. In most states a compliance test runs around $500 per batch regardless of size, and the testing minimum is typically 500 grams. Test a 300-gram batch and you have paid the full fee for 60% of the product. So nobody runs batches under 500 grams, and most programs prefer around 1,000.

The wash cooperates. One ice-water wash of fresh frozen generally lands near 500 grams of bubble hash. Nobody stops between washes on the same strain, so two or three washes get combined into a single 1,000- to 1,500-gram batch and tested once.

Equipment sets the top end. A wash vessel has an input capacity — one common commercial unit takes about 17,000 grams of fresh frozen — and at a 3–4% yield that is roughly one batch of hash per fill. Planning starts from the machine, not from a guess.

Three rules fall out of this:

- **Never test under 500 grams.** Combine washes until you clear it.
- **Plan in 500-gram steps.** Demand math — how many grams of rosin do I need — rounds up to the next increment, then converts back to pounds of biomass.
- **Keep the size the same run to run.** Consistent batches make yield comparisons between strains and suppliers mean something. A 4% result on a 1,500-gram batch and a 4% result on a 400-gram batch are not the same information.

Three recent batches from one program, each from a full ~17 kg fill of the same strain, finished at 474, 502, and 497 grams. That spread is what a standardized batch looks like. When the number jumps, you know it was the material, not the method.
